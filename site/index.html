Design notes

Who this is for

The client is building for young adults aging out of foster care. Support ends on a birthday — generally the eighteenth or the twenty-first — and what ends with it isn't only housing. It's the informal scaffolding most people that age still have: a parent's spare room to land in, someone to co-sign a lease, someone who notices when you go quiet for a week.

That makes the design brief an unusual one. Not supervised housing, and not an apartment block where nobody learns your name. Independence and company at the same time: a home of your own with a door you lock, and a porch that puts you three seconds from a conversation on the days you want one.

Every deliberate choice in this study is working on that. The eight-foot porch depth, the thirty-inch rise, the distance across the green, the paths that run between front doors rather than around them — all of it is trying to make being known optional but easy.

The porch is the whole idea

Each home turns its front door toward the same green. The porches are deep enough to actually live on — eight feet, room for two chairs and a person walking past to stop and lean on the rail — and they sit thirty inches above grade, so someone sitting is at eye level with someone standing on the path.

That is the whole social mechanic of the village: you can be at home and in company at the same time, without deciding to be. A closed door is never rude here, and an open one is never an obligation.

What you're looking at

Home 01 — board & batten A rocking chair and a fern that somebody remembers to water. Vertical siding, deep green door.
Home 02 — the swing A hanging porch swing facing the green, lap siding, mustard door. The one people gather on.

Same footprint, same porch, three different hands. Individual, permanent, cared for — never a row of identical units.

Light does the emotional work

Move the slider past 7:00 pm. The windows warm before the sky lets go, the porch lights come on one at a time, and the string lights draw the green closed into a room. Same geometry, entirely different feeling — which is why time of day is a control here and not a decision made once in a static image.

How this becomes the final deliverable

This is the concept stage: massing, siting, porch relationships, material palette, light. It exists to be argued with quickly — move a home, widen a porch, change a door color, see it in a second.

Once the composition is settled, it goes to high-resolution rendering: real materials and entourage, depth of field, the same camera framing and sunlight you approved here, matched to the quality standard of the Amphitheatre rendering. Stills for the donor deck, plus an optional walk-through animation on the arrival path.

About this study

Built as a working draft from the client's brief. Everything here — the homes, the trees, the porch furniture, the sky — is generated in code, so nothing is locked. Every dimension above is a variable waiting on MK's feedback.

Front Porch Village · Concept Study 01

The dignity of a front door of your own.

The client builds for young adults aging out of foster care — people whose support ends on a birthday, and who often don't have the spare room to land in that most of us did.

So the village has to do two things at once: give each resident a home that is unmistakably their own, and put their front doors close enough together that being known is easy. Three tiny homes, three porches, one shared green — walk around it, and watch it from first coffee to porch light.
